Privacy policy

Last updated: 26 November 2026

This policy covers the Tinkrum app for Android and iPhone (package name and bundle ID dev.stonetech.tinkrum) and this website. Tinkrum is published by Stonetech (“we”). Privacy contact: xia.pengda@gmail.com.

In short

  • Your entries, files, recognised text and recordings are stored and processed on your phone. Tinkrum does not upload them to us or to an AI service. They leave your phone only when you send or save them somewhere yourself.
  • When an AI changes your app, it receives your request and how your app is built, not your entries or files.
  • Tinkrum has no account, no ads, and no analytics or crash-reporting service of its own.
  • On Android, Tinkrum includes Google ML Kit, which sends Google technical measurements about its use. Details below.

What stays on your phone

The tables and entries you create, your photos, documents, videos and audio, the text read from them, speech turned into text, the suggestions made for new entries (including those of the optional on-device model), what Tinkrum remembers to improve those suggestions, your app’s settings and their history, and your saved presets.

Tinkrum asks the system to leave its data out of iCloud, computer and Google Drive backups. If you lose your phone, the data is lost unless you exported a backup yourself. An exported backup file is not encrypted: keep it somewhere safe.

Asking an AI to change your app

You can ask for changes in your own words. What happens depends on the choice under Settings → Tink and on the version of the app you have. Nothing is sent until you ask for a change.

Tinkrum’s built-in rules, which also run the introduction and its suggested sentences, work on your phone and send nothing.

Another AI app that you choose

Tinkrum opens your phone’s share sheet with your request, instructions for the AI, and a copy of how your app (or the whole of Tinkrum) is set up. The copy includes names of apps, pages, tables, fields and choices, text on your pages, and text you typed into settings, such as a watermark or stamp text. It contains no entries and no files. The app you pick receives it and handles it under its own privacy policy. Tinkrum reads your clipboard only when you tap to paste the answer.

Tink, through the Tinkrum server

Before the first request goes through our server, the app asks for your permission and names Anthropic as the recipient. If you say no, nothing is sent. Choosing another option under Settings → Tink withdraws the permission, and the app asks again if you come back.

When Tink uses the Tinkrum server, your phone sends:

  • your request, and earlier messages and replies from the same chat;
  • how your app is set up: names of apps, pages, tables, fields and choices, text on your pages, and settings of quick actions and reminders. References to your files, and text typed into settings marked private (such as a watermark), are replaced with placeholders;
  • where you opened Tink from (for example a page or a quick action; for shared files, only their types and number), and your language;
  • a random identifier that Tinkrum creates on your phone (it is not derived from your device or your identity), your platform and the app version.

Your entries, files, file names, recognised text and recordings are not sent. Anything you write in the request itself, or in names and page text, is sent, so leave private details out.

Our server forwards the request to Anthropic, whose Claude models write the change, and returns the reply. Our server does not save the text of requests or replies. It uses the random identifier to limit how much each installation can use the service, and writes a technical log with a shortened one-way hash of that identifier, the size, cost and duration of each request, and errors. The log contains no request or reply text and no IP address. We keep it for no longer than 12 months, then delete it.

Traffic to our server passes through Cloudflare, which processes it, including your IP address, to deliver and protect the connection.

Anthropic states that it deletes API inputs and outputs within 30 days, with exceptions: for example when required by law or to enforce its usage policy (content flagged by its safety systems may be kept for up to 2 years, and safety scores for up to 7 years). Anthropic states that by default it does not use API data to train its models. See Anthropic’s data retention and model training pages and its privacy policy.

Tink with your own API key

Your phone sends the request and app setup described above, without the Tinkrum identifier, directly to the provider you set up: Anthropic, or the compatible service whose address you entered. It does not pass through our server. That provider’s terms and privacy policy apply. Your key is kept in your phone’s secure storage and is sent only to that provider; testing the connection or listing models also contacts it.

“Tell us you need this”

When Tink cannot do something, you may see a button to tell us. Only if you tap it, our server receives and stores that one sentence, your language, why it could not be done, which kind of AI you use, your platform and the app version, with the time. The random identifier is used to limit repeated sending and is not stored with the sentence. No entries, files or app setup are sent. We keep stored sentences for no longer than 12 months, then delete them. You can hide the button under Settings → Tink.

Downloads

Tinkrum downloads some things when needed, such as extra tools, language data and models, and may check our list of downloadable tools when it starts (at most once a day). These requests carry none of your content and not the random identifier. Depending on the item, they go to our file server (morph-tools.stonetech.dev, on Cloudflare), jsDelivr, Hugging Face, Google or Apple. As with any download, the server sees your IP address and basic technical details of the request.

Google components on Android

On Android, Tinkrum uses Google ML Kit and Google Play services for features such as scanning documents, reading codes, translation, language identification, face framing for ID photos, background removal, recognising some kinds of text, and picking out details such as dates. Google states that the images and text you process stay on your device and are not sent to Google by ML Kit.

Google also states that ML Kit sends it measurements for diagnostics and usage analytics, and to maintain, improve and prevent abuse of these components: device information (such as maker, model and system version), the app’s package name and version, identifiers for the installation (for some features also a device identifier), performance figures, feature settings (for translation and language identification, the languages involved), input and output sizes, event types and error codes. Google states that this data is encrypted in transit and not transferred to third parties. ML Kit may also contact Google for updates and compatibility information. Tinkrum has no setting that turns this off.

See Google’s ML Kit data disclosure, ML Kit terms and privacy policy.

Reading text aloud uses the text-to-speech engine installed on your phone, and printing uses your phone’s print services. Their providers’ terms apply.

Apple components on iPhone

On iPhone, Tinkrum uses features built into iOS, such as scanning documents, reading text in images, turning speech into text and translation. The images, audio and text are processed on your phone; Tinkrum asks for speech to text to be done on the phone only. iOS may download language data from Apple for these features. Apple’s privacy policy applies to its system services.

What you send yourself

You can share, print, save to your photo library or a folder, export entries or backups, and open links. These happen only when you do them. Whatever receives the data (another app, a cloud drive, a website, a printer) handles it under its own terms.

We do not sell personal data or use it for advertising.

Permissions

  • Camera: to take photos and scan documents.
  • Microphone: to record audio when you start a recording, including while it continues in the background.
  • Speech recognition (iPhone): to turn recordings into text on your phone.
  • Photos and videos: to work with the ones you choose and to save results to your library when you ask. On Android, Tinkrum does not ask to read your photo library: you pick items in the system’s pickers or share them to Tinkrum. On Android 12 and earlier it also lists the older storage permissions, which saving can need.
  • Notifications: for your reminders and the progress of long tasks.
  • Network, background work and start-up (Android): for the connections described here, to finish long tasks, and to restore your reminders after the phone restarts.

Tinkrum does not ask for your location or contacts. You can change permissions in your phone’s settings; features that need one will then not work.

Keeping and deleting your data

On your phone

  • Data stays until you delete it. A deleted entry is kept under Settings → Data → Recently deleted for 30 days, then removed for good the next time the app starts.
  • Delete files under Settings → Storage. You can clear what Tinkrum remembers for suggestions under Settings → Data, and delete your API key under Settings → Tink.
  • On Android, uninstalling Tinkrum removes the data it holds on the phone.
  • On iPhone, deleting the app removes its data, but the system’s secure storage (Keychain) may keep the random identifier, your AI settings and any API key. Delete your key in Tinkrum first.
  • Copies you saved, shared or exported elsewhere are not removed.

With us and others

To ask for a copy or the deletion of what our server holds, write to xia.pengda@gmail.com. We have no account that links stored data to you, so tell us what you sent and roughly when. For data held by Anthropic, Google or another AI provider, their policies linked above also apply.

Security

Connections to our server, to Anthropic and for downloads use HTTPS. A service of your own must use HTTPS too, unless it runs on the phone itself. API keys are kept in the phone’s secure storage, and our own AI key stays on the server. No method of storage or transmission is completely secure.

This website

This website sets no cookies, has no trackers and loads nothing from other servers. It is hosted by Cloudflare, which processes your IP address and technical request data to deliver it, and may receive error reports from your browser if a page fails to load. The app-file checker runs in your browser. On the “Open in Tinkrum” page, the app is carried in the part of the link after #, which your browser does not send to the server. Links to other sites, such as GitHub, follow their own policies.

Changes

We will update this page and its date when what Tinkrum does with data changes. If our server is ever to save the text of AI requests or replies, this page will say so before that starts.
